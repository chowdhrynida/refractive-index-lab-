Measurement of Refractive Index of Liquid using LASER

Aim

To determine the refractive index of the given liquid using the LASER board method.

Apparatus

LASER board (porcelain container), He-Ne LASER, vernier callipers, given liquid.

Theory

This experiment works on the principle of total internal reflection. Fig (a) shows refraction of a ray of light travelling from a denser medium (say a liquid) to a rarer medium (say air). The ray bends away from the normal, so the angle of refraction r is greater than the angle of incidence i. If we keep on increasing i, at some point the angle r will reach 90° (parallel to the boundary). If i is increased beyond this point, the ray can no longer be refracted and is reflected back into the same medium (here, the liquid) with the angle of reflection equal to the angle of incidence.

For ordinary refraction from liquid to air, the angles of incidence and refraction are related by Snell's law:

μ = sin r / sin i … (1)

where μ is the refractive index of the liquid with respect to air. If we define the critical angle ic as the angle of incidence beyond which total internal reflection occurs, then r = 90° and eq. (1) becomes

μ = sin 90° / sin ic = 1 / sin ic

So if the angle ic is known, we can directly find the refractive index of the liquid. The critical angle can be determined experimentally using a simple arrangement of a LASER beam and a liquid container with a scattering bottom. If a porcelain container (LASER board) is used, its white bottom acts as an excellent scatterer. The arrangement is shown in Fig (b).

When the LASER beam is incident on the bottom, it scatters light in all directions. This results in a dark circle being formed at the centre. This can be explained using Fig (c): of all the light rays scattered towards the surface, those incident on the surface at an angle less than ic are refracted out and are not reflected back. Since no light is reflected back to the bottom for these angles, the region near the scattering centre is not illuminated and appears dark. Beyond the angle ic, all rays are totally internally reflected back to the bottom, making this region appear bright. From the geometry of Fig (c) we can write

i_c = tan⁻¹( (R/2) / h )

The radius of the dark circle can be determined by placing a graph paper at the bottom of the container, with a small hole at the centre of the sheet for the scattering centre. The height h of the liquid from the bottom can be measured accurately using the vernier callipers.

Formula

  1. Critical angle   ic = tan⁻¹( R / 2h ) = tan⁻¹( D / 4h ),   where D is the diameter of the dark circle (D = 2R).
  2. Refractive index   μ = 1 / sin ic

Error analysis (uncertainty)

Every measurement has an uncertainty. Here Δh = 0.01 cm (least count of the vernier) and ΔR is the uncertainty in reading R from the loupe grid (the grid is in 1 mm divisions and the edge of the dark circle is slightly diffuse, so you may estimate R to a fraction of a division). Since sin ic = R / √(R² + 4h²), we have μ = √(1 + 4h²/R²), and combining the errors in quadrature gives

Δμ = ((μ² − 1) / μ) · √[ (Δh/h)² + (ΔR/R)² ]

For the graph, draw the steepest and the flattest line through the origin that still passes through the error bars (R ± ΔR) of every point:

Smax = min[(Ri + ΔR)/hi]  ;  Smin = max[(Ri − ΔR)/hi]  ;  μ(S) = √(1 + 4/S²)  ;  Δμgraph = |μ(Smin) − μ(Smax)| / 2

Finally report μ = μ̄ ± Δμ̄ and the percentage uncertainty = (Δμ̄ / μ̄) × 100, where Δμ̄ is the mean of the Δμ values of the individual readings. The standard deviation (SD) of your μ values tells you how well the readings agree with each other.

Procedure

  1. Arrange the apparatus as shown in Fig (b).
  2. The height h of the liquid is determined using the vernier callipers.
  3. The radius R of the dark circle is read off along the two directions (ruling on the graph paper) and the mean radius is determined.
  4. Repeat steps 2 and 3 for different heights of the liquid level.
  5. The liquid (here, water) is replaced by another liquid (salt water / paraffin oil) and the experiment is repeated.

Precautions

  1. Never look directly into the LASER beam and never point it towards anyone's face.
  2. Before changing the liquid, empty the container and wipe it clean with tissue; otherwise the liquids mix and μ will come out wrong.
  3. Make the beam fall exactly on the hole (scattering centre) of the graph paper.
  4. Read the edge of the dark circle carefully with the magnifier/loupe, and read the vernier carefully.
